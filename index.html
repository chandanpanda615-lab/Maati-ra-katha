East to west

The whole village lies along one road, and every day crosses it.

Morning

East is the fields. The light gets there first.

Open cultivated land runs out to the north and east of the village. The soil under it is deep and fine, clay enough to hold monsoon water for paddy.

A day here starts on that side, before you are ready for it.

Day

The market road is the spine.

About a kilometre of it, east to west, with the market in the middle. The road in arrives from the south, from the Baliguda side.

Most of what happens in a day happens somewhere along it.

Evening

West is the forest. The sun goes down into it.

The ground rises to the south-west into hills and valleys, scrub and forest, climbing on into the Eastern Ghats.

The village sits between the two: fields on the side where the day begins, forest on the side where it ends.

Night

A fire, and whoever is standing round it.

That is all the photograph shows, and all this page will claim. The rest of the night is for the week itself.

The stories the soil tells

There are hundreds of stories here. They don’t happen in grand, orchestrated events. They happen quietly at dawn, when the mist still hangs over the hills.

Why I am building this slowly
